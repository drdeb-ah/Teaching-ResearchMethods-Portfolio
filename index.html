Gathering Data Like a Scientist

Operationalizing the Construct of CURIOSITY

📏 The Prompt

For anyone "Gathering Data like a Scientist," there must be a clear idea of how to operationalize and measure CONSTRUCTS within the experiment — especially constructs like CURIOSITY.

Identify in what way you would operationalize the construct of CURIOSITY and WHAT to MEASURE to collect:

  • One PHYSIOLOGICAL data point (identify the data you are collecting)

Class tie-in required: connect your operational definition and your measure to at least one construct, study or measurement activity we covered in class, and explain how it shaped your choices.

Part A — Operationalizing CURIOSITY
How would you define and operationalize the construct of CURIOSITY for a scientific study?
Operationalizing means defining the construct in a specific, measurable way. How would you know curiosity is present or absent, or high versus low? What observable indicators would you use?
Class example: Which construct or measurement example from class guided how you operationalized CURIOSITY?
Part B — Physiological Data Point
What physiological measurement would you collect to measure CURIOSITY?
Physiological = body-based measurement. Examples: heart rate, skin conductance (EDA/GSR), fMRI activation, eye tracking, pupil dilation, EEG, cortisol levels, etc.

Original Research Articles

📝 Annotation Format (200 words per source)

Part 1 (~150 words): Paraphrase the experimental hypothesis, logic behind the hypothesis, methods used, and major result of the experiment.

Part 2 (~50 words): Describe HOW and WHY this paper supports the hypothesis you are testing. Note how our class study differs from this one (participants, location, questionnaires, etc.). Highlight any gaps or improvements our study could address.

Do NOT use direct quotes. Paraphrase only. Include a full APA 7th edition citation.

📄 APA Research Paper Requirements

Your paper must be written in Microsoft Word (.docx), formatted according to APA 7th Edition:

  • Double-spaced throughout with 1-inch margins
  • Typed in 12-point Times New Roman font
  • Written in your own words — no direct quotes; paraphrase only

Required sections and minimum lengths:

  • Title Page
  • Abstract — maximum 300 words
  • Introduction — minimum 3 full pages
  • Method — minimum 2 pages (Participants, Measures, Procedure subsections)
  • Results — minimum 2 pages
  • Discussion — minimum 3 full pages
  • References — 10 peer-reviewed sources (8 original research, 2 review articles)
  • Tables
  • Appendices (as needed)
